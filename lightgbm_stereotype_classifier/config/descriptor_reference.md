# Descriptor reference of the LightGBM stereotype classifier

This file documents the 301 descriptors used by the classifier: the 285 descriptors of the multi-class model (`baseline_feature_order` in `feature_columns.json`) and the 29 descriptors of the Walnut expert (`specialist_feature_order`), 13 of which are shared by both models. The same content is available as `descriptor_reference.csv` (UTF-8, separator `;`), one row per descriptor, in the order of `descriptor_provenance.csv`.

## Columns

- `descriptor`: column name in the feature table.
- `family`: descriptor family (as in `descriptor_families.csv`).
- `used_by`: `multi-class`, `Walnut expert` or `both`.
- `temporal_scope`: frames the value depends on (see below).
- `description`: what is measured and how it is normalised, written from the code that computes it (`feature_extraction/`).
- `method_reference` and `doi`: published method the descriptor directly relies on (see below).
- `script`: computation script, as in `descriptor_provenance.csv`.

## Temporal scopes

| Temporal scope | Descriptors | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| single frame | 220 | computed on the labelled frame T0 only (mask, raw frame or 384 px formatted image) |
| three-frame window | 71 | computed on T-1, T0, T+1 (masks, raw frames, aligned frames or the registered motion image); at the first or last frame of a clip the window slides to three real frames on one side |
| eleven-frame window (+/-5) | 4 | computed on the masks from T-5 to T+5 (at least 4 masks) |
| all frames of the worm | 4 | aggregated over every frame of the same worm; the same value is assigned to all its frames, so the full sequence of the worm is required |
| five-frame window (+/-2) | 2 | computed on the masks from T-2 to T+2 (at least 3 masks) |

Three descriptors of the motion group read only the current channel of the registered motion image (`rm_center_thick`, `ip_intensity_cv`, `ip_edge_p95`) and `body_lat_wave` uses only the T0 mask, so they are labelled single frame, although they are produced together with three-frame descriptors.

## Inputs and normalisation rules

- Masks are produced by the Mask2Former segmenter; the largest connected component is used.
- "384 px mask-constrained image" is the T0 frame cropped on its mask and formatted to 384 px with the background masked (FlatMorphAI formatter). "384 px formatted T0 silhouette" is the set of pixels brighter than 8 in that image.
- There is no pixel-to-millimetre scale, so lengths are divided by sqrt(mask area), by the equivalent radius, by the median body width or by the body length, areas by the mask area, and intensities by the body mean, median, standard deviation or a percentile range. Counts, angles and correlations are dimensionless. A few values remain in pixel units of a resampled image (for example the curvatures in 1/pixel of the 384 px image or the optical flow in pixels per frame of the aligned frame); the description states it.
- 14 descriptors that remained correlated with worm size were residualised against log(mask area + 1): value - (b0 + b1 x log(area + 1)), with b0 and b1 fitted on the training set (`size_normalize_metrics.py`) and applied unchanged to new data (`apply_size_residuals.py`). Their description ends with "Residualised against log(mask area + 1)".
- Centerline descriptors use the longest path of the skeleton of the mask; head and tail are not identified, so most end-related descriptors are taken as the maximum, the minimum or the asymmetry of the two ends.

## How references and DOIs were chosen

A reference is given only when the descriptor directly applies a published method, and only from a fixed list of verified references: Zhang and Suen 1984 (skeleton), Hu 1962 (moment invariants), Haralick et al. 1973 (GLCM), Ojala et al. 2002 (LBP), Galloway 1975 (run lengths), Frangi et al. 1998 (Frangi filter), Meijering et al. 2004 (Meijering filter), Zuiderveld 1994 (CLAHE), Zabrodsky et al. 1995 (symmetry), Stephens et al. 2008 (midline profiles), Farneback 2003 (dense optical flow, only when `cv2.calcOpticalFlowFarneback` is called), Daugman 1985 (Gabor filters) and Kass and Witkin 1987 (structure tensor). When two methods are both essential (for example a skeleton and a profile along it), both are given, joined by "+", in the same order in `method_reference` and `doi`. Other established methods (Zernike moments, elliptic Fourier descriptors, Laws texture energy, Sato filter, Hessian ridge filter, morphological black-hat, box-counting fractal dimension, curvature scale space, thin-plate spline) are named without a DOI. Everything else is marked `project-specific` with an empty DOI. 154 descriptors carry at least one DOI.
